Answer bids with proof, not promises.

Bid Analyser is a skills-based AI agent kit for product managers. It checks every RFP requirement and every seller claim against your official product documentation, and it keeps a trustworthy record of the gaps that cost you deals.

Why I built it

Bids fail in the same four ways.

Product managers get pulled into bids late, with little time to check them. The same mistakes repeat from one bid to the next, and nobody counts what they cost.

×

Sellers over-commit

A draft bid promises a feature, a platform, a discount or a roadmap date that product never approved.

×

Claims have no evidence

"Fully supported" ends up in the response because it sounds right, not because a document says so.

×

Integrations are assumed

Two products from the same vendor are described as one integrated flow, though one hop was never verified.

×

Gaps are never counted

The same missing capability loses deal after deal, but there is no log to show product leadership how often.

What it is

Two modes. Six skills. One rule: cite it or don't claim it.

It is a set of prompts and skill definitions with two small Python helpers, not an application. You load it into any agent platform that supports system instructions, skills and file tools.

Mode A

Customer RFP / RFI

Procurement documents your company must answer. You get fit, gaps, risks and response priorities, ending in a bid-health verdict: Strong, Viable, At risk or No-bid.

Mode B

Seller bid review

A bid drafted by a seller. It catches over-commitments, wrong licensing and unsupported claims before the bid reaches the customer. The verdict is Approve, Approve with changes, Reject or Needs more info.

Source hierarchy: highest wins

The agent never uses its general knowledge as evidence.

1
Official product documentation for the exact version you assess. The only source that can prove a capability.
2
Claims Register. Your approved list of differentiators and integrations. Only claims marked Verified reach response wording.
3
Licensing, pricing and approval rules.
4
The bid or RFP itself. It defines what is asked or promised, never what the product does.
5
Web research. Always flagged with the URL and date, and it can only support "Needs Evidence".
How it works

A pipeline that stops when evidence runs out.

Skills run in a fixed order. If a skill fails or its input is missing, the workflow stops and reports it instead of continuing on partial data.

1
client-bid-workspace

Names the client workspace and plans the filing folders, from Intake to Archive.

Always
2
rfp-requirements-extraction

Builds one traceable register of requirements, evaluation model, compliance mandates and key dates. No fit judgement.

Always
3
capability-mapping

Maps each requirement to a capability with a documentation citation and a fit classification. Flags seller over-commitments.

Always
Evidence gate. No product documentation retrieved? The analysis stops. Nothing is guessed.
4
competitive-defensibility

Evidence-backed differentiators, where competitors are stronger, claims to remove, and trap questions.

If competitors appear
5
ecosystem-integration-fit

Splits each integration into hops and classifies mechanism, licence need and effort. A story is only as strong as its weakest hop.

If integrations appear
6
capability-gap-capture

Collects gaps, deduplicates them against a cross-bid log, and writes only what a human approves.

Always last

Every requirement gets exactly one label

Each label carries a confidence rating: High (cited), Medium (reasoned from cited facts) or Low (assumption, with what would resolve it).

MeetsA documentation citation explicitly describes it.
Partially MeetsSupported in part. The missing piece is stated.
GapNot supported, or only on the roadmap.
Needs EvidencePlausible, but no citation was found.
Needs ClarificationThe requirement itself is ambiguous.
Design principles

Built to be trusted by the people who review it.

Evidence over assertion

Seller claims, file names, metadata, earlier analyses and model knowledge are never proof.

Fail closed

If product evidence can't be retrieved, the analysis stops instead of guessing.

Human approval

Nothing is written to the gap log without explicit approval. No gap is ever promised or dated.

Confidential by default

Summaries say "the customer". Quotes are capped. Client names never appear in roadmap exports.

Inputs, not approvals

Legal, contractual, security and pricing conclusions are inputs for review, never sign-off.

Same answer shape

Nine fixed sections, from one-line verdict to proposed gaps, so reviews are comparable across bids.

How to use it

Set up in six steps.

The kit ships no product knowledge. The quality of the output depends on the documentation and Claims Register you give it.

  1. Pick an agent platformAny platform that supports a system prompt, skills (or equivalent tools) and file access. The skills assume code-interpreter and document-search capabilities.
  2. Replace the placeholdersIn agent/instructions.md and the skills, replace <PRODUCT> and <VERSION> with your product name and the documentation version you assess against.
  3. Load your knowledgeAdd your product documentation for that version, a Claims Register (each claim marked Verified, Unverified or Rejected), and your licensing, pricing and approval rules.
  4. Define your capability taxonomyEdit skills/capability-mapping/SKILL.md. It ships as a placeholder for you to fill in.
  5. Register the skillsAdd each skills/<name>/ folder to your agent. Keep the scripts/ folders next to their SKILL.md.
  6. Adapt the exportChange the CSV columns in capability-gap-capture to match your roadmap or idea-management tool.

Then talk to it

Mode A

"Here is a new RFP from a customer. Analyse it against our product docs."

Mode B

"Review this seller's draft bid for over-commitments before it goes out."

Single question

"Does the product meet this requirement? Give me the classification and safe response wording."

Log a gap

"Log a gap: customers keep asking for X." This runs the gap skill on its own, with no bid needed.

Try the helper scripts

Both use only the Python 3 standard library and read JSON from stdin.

# Plan the filing workspace for a bid
echo '{"client": "Example Corp", "files": ["Example RFP v2.pdf", "Addendum 1.pdf"]}' \
  | python3 skills/client-bid-workspace/scripts/main.py

# Suggest merges against the existing gap log
echo '{"existing": [{"gap_id": "G001", "need": "Single sign-on with SAML"}],
       "new": [{"req_id": "R12", "need": "Support SAML single sign-on"}]}' \
  | python3 skills/capability-gap-capture/scripts/gap_log.py

Known limitations

Output quality depends entirely on the documentation and Claims Register you supply.

Filing is routed by filename keywords, so ambiguous names land in UNCLASSIFIED for a person to resolve.

Gap matching is token-based and won't catch synonyms. A person confirms every merge.

Quote limits differ by stage (25 words in analysis, 40 in extraction). Align them to your confidentiality policy.
